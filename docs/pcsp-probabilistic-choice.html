Probabilistic choice in PCSP

  • Internal choice (CSP): P ⊓ Q — the system may take either branch, and the choice is made inside the process in a way the outside world cannot observe or control—like a hidden scheduler picking one of two options.
  • External choice (CSP): P □ Q — the process offers several alternatives and waits until the environment (another process, a user, or an input event) selects which branch to take— for example, which communication guard or input becomes ready first.
  • Probabilistic choice (pCSP): P p⊕ Q — when the choice is resolved, process P is taken with probability p and process Q with probability 1 − p, as if a weighted coin were flipped at that moment.

In PCSP, explicit probability is introduced to model randomized algorithms or unpredictable environments. This adds a third distinct choice operator:

Probabilistic choice (P p⊕ Q, or P p(+) Q): this operator resolves branching by selecting process P with probability p and process Q with probability 1 − p, where p ∈ [0, 1].

Semantic and algebraic properties

Coexistence with nondeterminism

A core challenge in PCSP is the interaction between internal choice (P ⊓ Q) and probabilistic choice (P p⊕ Q). Internal choice represents total uncertainty; probabilistic choice represents quantifiable risk.

Distributivity

In refinement-oriented models of PCSP, the probabilistic choice operator distributes through standard CSP operators, including nondeterministic choice. For example:

P p⊕ (Q ⊓ R) ≡ (P p⊕ Q) ⊓ (P p⊕ R)

Testing preorders

In PCSP verification (for example in tools such as the PAT toolkit), semantic models use probabilistic testing preorders (may- and must-testing). Refinement checking asks whether an implementation matches a specification up to a calculated probability threshold.
