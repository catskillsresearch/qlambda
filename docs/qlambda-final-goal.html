Final goal

Give qλ and IBM Quantum Composer a single, compositional classical–quantum denotational semantics, and prove that compilation and embedding preserve that meaning—not merely that two graphs look the same.

Concretely

  1. One semantic object. For fixed finite quantum and classical registers, define a shared domain DCQ (CP maps, measurement instruments, classical store updates, control flow). The meaning of a circuit is ⟦C⟧ ∈ DCQ, built compositionally from gates and control structure. OpenQASM and the Composer diagram are export syntax, not the definition of semantics.
  2. One source language. Small call-by-value qλ with ⊕, ⊓, □ and emit I against a frozen Composer instruction AST, plus opaque handles and a separate store. Program meaning ⟦M⟧ uses Scott-style domains for the functional/classical layer and the same CQ layer for each emit.
  3. Two directions, one equation. Total embed Composer → qλ; compile well-formed terminating programs → one Composer circuit. The capstone theorems are denotation preservation:

    ⟦compile(M)⟧ = ⟦M⟧

    (with scheduler/selector parameters where ⊓ and □ require them), and round-trip on the defined fragment up to normalization—not “AST equality” as the main story.

  4. Honest scope. Ideal, finite-register, noise-free semantics aligned with QLambda/QuantumInstrument.lean; Chen–Kou–Lyu / ωFVA where it applies to classical higher-order probability; no claim that ideal denotation equals a noisy QPU or arbitrary Qiskit Python.

Formalize quantum circuits as elements of a classical–quantum instrument domain, give qλ the same denotation story, and machine-check that compiling functional programs with three choice operators into Composer preserves that semantics—so domain theory and quantum CP semantics meet in one proved pipeline, with Composer as a verified front-end to IBM’s workflow.
